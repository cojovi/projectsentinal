**Correction and historical follow-up — October 28, 2026.** Anthropic's June suspension statement supports the historical access disruption. Its June 30 redeployment announcement set a July 1 return for Fable 5. The earlier article described several unsupported procedural claims as invented or wrong too categorically. This version distinguishes **absent from the reviewed sources** from **proved false everywhere**. It also removes an unsupported comparative benchmark assertion and any suggestion of independent recording or government-directive authentication.

A story about a temporary model suspension can accumulate a second story about secret policy machinery. The extra detail may sound authoritative precisely because it is specific. Specificity is useful only when a source can carry it.

## What the reviewed record supports

[Anthropic's suspension statement](https://www.anthropic.com/news/fable-mythos-access) says a US export-control directive required it to suspend Fable 5 and Mythos 5 access for foreign nationals. The company said complying meant disabling access for all customers, while other models remained unaffected.

That is a substantive historical event in the company's published account. It should not be dismissed as a fabricated June episode. It should also not be upgraded into our independent authentication of the directive or its technical rationale.

The [June 9 launch material](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-fable-5-mythos-5) describes the company's release and safeguards approach. Its performance and safety claims remain vendor claims here. This review did not reproduce the benchmarks or jailbreak tests.

The correction is deliberately bounded. We can say these sources do not substantiate a claim. We cannot turn a limited document check into universal proof of nonexistence.

## The return changes the chronology

[Anthropic's June 30 post](https://www.anthropic.com/news/redeploying-fable-5) said export controls had been lifted and Fable 5 would return globally on July 1. It separately described restricted Mythos access and further coordination. The captured launch page carries a July 1 availability update.

The original June 18 publication preceded those announcements. Its historical date remains; this dated follow-up supplies the later context. Neither the initial suspension nor the later return demonstrates the weight-handover, inspector or percentage-cap claims.

See the [June 13 suspension account and follow-up](/post/2026-06-13-anthropics-fable-5-lasted-three-days-before-washington-yanked-it-offline) for the access timeline.

## Precision is a governance demand — analysis

The policy questions remain serious: what legal authority supports a restriction, what technical evidence triggers it, and what process restores access? An opaque intervention can create uncertainty for customers and developers. An inadequately justified release can create different risks. Neither concern supplies missing terms in an agreement.

A useful account asks for the directive, the mechanism, the scope and the remediation path. A benchmark number requires a test definition and conditions; an inspection power requires an authority and jurisdiction. A company account can establish what the company says without resolving every side of the dispute.

The same standard should apply to optimistic competitive forecasts. A temporary setback for one provider is not proof of another lab's capabilities, prices or delivery schedule. Those require their own evidence.
